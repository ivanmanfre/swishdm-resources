How to make a useful page edit

Write the fact a buyer needs. If you sell a 35L backpack, state the capacity and weight for that version. One familiar unit is enough; another unit can make comparison easier.

Explain a limitation when it matters. If the bag does not expand or the hip belt is sold separately, say that clearly. A clear “no” still answers the question.

Put the information beside the buying decision. Add the detail to a product specification, feature description or relevant FAQ. Keep it in readable text and check that the page's product data agrees.

Check the facts before publishing. Confirm dimensions, prices and warranty terms with the person who owns the product information. Recheck the page after editing; AI visibility needs its own repeated measurement over time.

A worked example, with a limited sample.

SwishDM's full research standard is at least 100 repeats of each question before forming an opinion. This pilot has fewer repeats per question. Use it to explore the page check and inspect the evidence. A larger study would firm up the percentages.

Which questions and AI tools did you use?

We used four shopping situations, each written as a full question and a short search phrase. Web captures requested a US search session. We kept API answers separate because they can differ from the apps shoppers use.

Google AI Overviews were not available for every run. Ten short captures were excluded. The remaining answers include repeats and are not independent observations of shopper behaviour.

How were the topics counted?

A model read each answer and extracted the product details used in the response, with a supporting quote. Similar details were grouped into these 14 topics. A topic counts once per answer, even if it appears several times. Every quote shown here is checked against the saved answer.

A separate model rechecked 120 sampled extractions and accepted 110. This measures agreement on the sampled extractions. It gives no confidence level for the findings. Topic grouping and model judgements can still be wrong.

The filters recalculate the count and denominator from the recorded answers. Frequency tells you what appeared in this sample; it does not reveal a model's internal ranking rules or prove which details caused a recommendation.

How is product-page coverage judged?

Clear: the captured page gives a concrete answer about the topic. Partial: it mentions the topic but leaves a basic fact unclear or inconsistent. Not found: we could not find an answer in the checked capture. Check capture: incomplete evidence prevents a fair judgement.

We check the specific product version, accept convertible units and count a clear statement that a feature is absent. A page does not need every optional feature in a topic. The count is unweighted: a clearly answered topic counts once.

The page grades were independently reviewed on 17 October. The original automated rubric was too demanding in places, so this report uses the revised evidence review. Optional improvements are separate from the basic coverage judgement.

What can the captures tell us?

Pages were fetched from Warsaw on 16 October. We read the returned HTML and product data. Osprey required a browser-rendered capture, while Cotopaxi's extracted text was incomplete. Those limits are shown alongside their findings.

These captures do not establish exactly what a search engine saw. A page may have information in another region, a linked policy or a section the extractor missed. Check the live page before changing it.

Prices, availability and airline rules change. AI answers may contain mistakes. Source excerpts are retained as evidence of what was said, with their original wording.

Why does it matter what my page says?

When AI tools compare products in a category, they judge them on the same few details again and again. If your page states those details in plain text, the tools have your own words to use when they describe and compare your product. If it leaves them out, the answer relies on reviews, retailers and other sites, or skips you in the comparison.

Nobody can promise a recommendation: reviews and other sources count too. Your product page is the part you control, so start there, then track how AI tools describe your product over time. Google's guidance on AI search also recommends making important content available as text and makes clear that inclusion is not guaranteed.
