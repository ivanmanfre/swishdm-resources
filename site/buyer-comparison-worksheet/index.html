How to run it

Six steps

  1. 01

    Choose the pair

    Your brand is A. The competitor is B. Pick a brand that sells to the same buyer, for the same use, at a similar price. The best choice is the one you hear about on sales calls or see in your reviews ("I almost bought B").

    Skip the category giant unless your customers really compare you with it. A mismatched pair gives you answers about price tier and little else.

  2. 02

    Pick three buyer questions

    Use the question bank below. Fill in the bracketed parts with your real use case, such as the skin type, the room, the activity or the country. Write them the way a customer would type them.

    Then freeze the wording. Every run uses the exact same text, so differences in the answers come from the assistant and the brand order.

    Leave yourself out of it. Never say you own either brand, and avoid loaded words such as "premium A or cheap B".

  3. 03

    Set up a clean session

    • ChatGPT: sign out, or use a temporary chat so saved memory and past chats stay out of the answer.
    • Gemini: use a new chat each time. If you are signed in, switch off personalisation or past-chat features where the settings offer them.
    • Perplexity: start a new thread each time. It shows its sources by default.

    Record in the log whether you were signed in, whether web search ran, and the country you asked from. Answers can change with each of these.

    Optional: Google AI Mode and Microsoft Copilot are worth adding once the first three are done. Use the same questions and add rows to the log.

  4. 04

    Ask in both orders, three fresh runs each

    Ask each question with your brand named first ("A first"), then with the competitor named first ("B first"). Do three runs of each, every run in a brand new chat. That is 18 answers per assistant.

    Flipping the order checks for a simple bias towards whichever brand appears first. Fresh chats stop one answer from shaping the next.

  5. 05

    Log every answer

    For each run, record the date and time, the verdict, the words the assistant used about each brand, and every page it cited. Copy the URLs exactly. Save the full answer too (a screenshot or pasted text) so you can check the log later.

    Verdict options: A favoured, B favoured, mixed (depends on the buyer, or no clear pick), neither (recommends a third brand or nothing), invalid (refused, confused the brand, answered a different question, errored).

  6. 06

    Read it, then pick the pages to fix

    Count the verdicts, collect the repeated words, and sort the cited pages by who owns them. The two guides further down cover how to read the results honestly and how to turn them into a short fix list.

Reading the results

Three things to pull from every answer

1. The verdict

Which brand did it lean towards, if any? Count every valid answer, including mixed and neither. Then compare the A first and B first runs for the same question.

2. The words

Copy the exact phrases used about each brand: what it says A and B are good at, what it warns about, who it says each one suits. Phrases that come back across runs and assistants are the ones buyers are most likely to read.

3. The pages behind it

For every cited URL, note who owns the page and its date if one is visible:

  • Your site
  • Competitor's site
  • Review site or listicle
  • Reddit, forum or YouTube
  • Retailer or marketplace
  • Press

Look for

  • Facts about you that are wrong or out of date.
  • Strengths of yours that never come up.
  • One page cited again and again.
  • Old reviews or threads still shaping the answer.
  • Your own pages missing from the citations.

Read it honestly

What this sample can and cannot tell you

It is a small sample

Eighteen answers per assistant is enough to spot patterns worth checking. Always write results as a count with its total ("_ of 18"), never as a percentage on its own.

Answers vary

The same question can get a different answer on another run, another day, another account or after a model update. A change next month can happen with nothing changed on your side.

Order can matter

If one brand only wins when it is named first, record the result as order sensitive and treat it with caution.

Keep every run

Mixed and neither are results. Count invalid runs separately with the reason. Rerun only to replace an invalid run, and note that you did.

Citations are partial

Cited pages show what the assistant pointed to in that answer. They may leave out other material it drew on.

It measures answers

This shows what assistants said in your sample. Rankings, traffic and sales need their own measurement. If your brand comes out well, that is your result too.

From log to action

Which pages to fix first

Start with the pages that show up most in your log. For each pattern below, check the first thing in the right column before you change anything.

If you see thisCheck this first
A wrong or outdated fact about you, with a cited pageOpen the cited page. If it is yours, update it. If it belongs to someone else, contact the publisher with the current facts and ask for an update.
A strength of yours that never comes upCheck whether your product and category pages state it in plain text: materials, ingredients, sizing, warranty, returns, shipping to the US and Canada.
The competitor's own comparison or "vs" page is citedRead what it says about you. Decide whether you need a fair comparison page of your own, and where independent coverage could carry the same facts.
Your reviews and ratings never come upCheck whether review text appears on the page as readable text, or only inside a widget that loads after the page.
The same review site, listicle or thread keeps appearingThat page is shaping the answer. Check its date, what it says about each brand, and whether you are in it at all.
A weakness that is trueLog it for the product or customer team. Your pages can explain the trade-off honestly and say who the product suits.
