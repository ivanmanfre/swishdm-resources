Key Buying Decisions Before You Order
  • Hardwired vs. Plug-In (NEMA 14-50):
  • Hardwired units connect directly to your electrical panel, allowing maximum charging speeds (48A–50A), cleaner weatherproofing for outdoor installs, and no need for an expensive GFCI breaker in many jurisdictions.
  • Plug-in units (NEMA 14-50 or 6-50) cap out at 40A continuous load (requiring a 50A circuit) and let you take the charger with you if you move.
  • Connector Type (J1772 vs. NACS): Most newer North American EVs (2025+) use NACS (the Tesla-style port), while older non-Tesla EVs use J1772 . Adapters work both ways, or you can buy a unit with the native plug your car uses.
  • Amperage & Panel Capacity: A 48A charger requires a dedicated 60-amp circuit (electrical code limits continuous loads to 80% of breaker capacity). If your home has a 100A service panel, look for a charger with dynamic load management (like Wallbox or Emporia with its Vue monitor) so you don't have to pay thousands for a panel upgrade.
  • Smart Wi-Fi Features Worth Using:
  • Off-peak scheduling: Automatically starts charging after midnight when utility Time-of-Use (TOU) rates drop.
  • Utility rebate eligibility: Many power companies offer $250–$1, 000+ rebates, but require you to buy a specific ENERGY STAR-certified Wi-Fi charger (ChargePoint, Emporia, and Wallbox are widely approved) so they can verify off-peak usage.
