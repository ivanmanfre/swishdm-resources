Standard "all-in-one" multivitamins rarely contain enough electrolytes to prevent the "keto flu, " simply because minerals like magnesium and potassium are physically too bulky to fit into one or two capsules. Instead, focus on these criteria:

  • Zero Sugar & Net Carbs: Avoid gummies, chewables, and liquid drops sweetened with cane sugar, dextrose, or maltodextrin. Look for capsules, tablets, or powders sweetened with stevia, monk fruit, or erythritol.
  • Bioavailable B-Complex: Cutting whole grains lowers thiamine (B1), riboflavin (B2), and folate (B9) intake. Look for methylated forms (like methylcobalamin for B12 and L-methylfolate for B9), which are easier for your body to absorb and utilize for energy metabolism.
  • Vitamin D3 + K2: Since keto is fat-rich, pairing Vitamin D3 with K2 (as MK-7) helps direct calcium to your bones rather than your arteries, and absorbs efficiently alongside your dietary fats.
  • Trace Minerals: Look for zinc, selenium, and chromium to support thyroid function, immune health, and insulin sensitivity.
  • Iron-Free (Usually): Unless you have a diagnosed iron deficiency, most people on keto get plenty of heme iron from red meat, poultry, and fish.

Don't Rely on a Multivitamin for Electrolytes

Because a capsule can only hold so much physical weight, even the best keto multivitamin will only give you around 50–100 mg of magnesium and almost no potassium or sodium. To avoid fatigue, muscle cramps, and headaches, pair your multivitamin with a dedicated electrolyte strategy:

  • Sodium: 3, 000–5, 000 mg/day (mostly via salting food or broth)
  • Potassium: 3, 000–4, 000 mg/day (avocados, leafy greens, salmon, or potassium chloride/NoSalt)
  • Magnesium: 300–400 mg/day (magnesium glycinate or citrate taken separately)

Tip for absorption: Take your multivitamin with your largest fat-containing meal of the day so fat-soluble vitamins ( A, D, E, and K ) absorb properly.
