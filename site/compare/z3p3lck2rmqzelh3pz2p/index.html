Because road tires run much higher pressures (60–90+ PSI) than mountain or gravel tires, they require a sealant that can plug a hole quickly before losing too much air.

Key Differences: Road vs. Gravel Demands

  • Road Tires (High Pressure, Low Volume): High pressure forces air and liquid out of a puncture with immense velocity, which can blow standard liquid sealant straight out of the hole before it clots. Road-specific or high-pressure formulas use finer sealing particles or specialized coagulants to plug leaks instantly at 60+ PSI.
  • Gravel Tires (Medium Pressure, High Volume): Running lower pressures (30–50 PSI) makes it easier for sealants to clot. However, gravel riding exposes tires to sharp flints, thorns, and pinch-flats, meaning you want a sealant with robust suspended particles (like rubber bits or fibers) to bridge larger cuts.

Maintenance Tips for Tubeless Setup

  • Top-Off Frequency: Liquid sealant dries out inside the tire over time. Check and replenish sealant every 2 to 6 months depending on your climate and riding frequency.
  • Valve Core Placement: Position your valve stem at the 4 o'clock or 8 o'clock position when injecting sealant through the valve core to prevent the liquid from immediately gumming up the valve mechanism.
  • CO2 Cartridges: Be cautious when using CO2 inflators to seat a tire; extreme cold from CO2 can freeze and ruin liquid latex sealants. It is best to seat the tire with a floor pump or compressor first, then add sealant through the valve core afterward if needed.
