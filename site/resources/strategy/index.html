01

In short

The message

Getting cited by AI is the easy part. What AI tells a buyer comparing you with a competitor is what wins or loses the sale.

The goal

When an ecommerce founder looks Jonathan up, they find a founder who does this work for real brands, and the results to prove it.

The reader

Founders and marketing heads of US and Canadian ecommerce brands doing $3m to $10m+ a year.

The posts

Five kinds of post, client stories first. About five a week.

Your time

Review a batch of about ten drafts every two weeks. Nothing goes out without your approval.

“A lot of brands are hyper focused on the amount of times that they're being cited, but very few of them are actually checking what AI says when they're showing up.”Jonathan, Shenzhen SEO, September 2026
02

How a buyer finds Jonathan

Most first conversations will come from our outreach. When a founder gets a message, they check Jonathan's profile. The posts are what they find there.

  1. 1A messageoffering a comparison of their brand and a close competitor in AI answers
  2. 2A profile visitthey check who sent it
  3. 3Posts and the free reportclient results, clear opinions, a report on their own brand
  4. 4A call with Jonathanwith a short brief on the brand beforehand

From a study of 6,454 posts by 82 creators in ecommerce SEO and AI search: posts with a real image or a document did about 60% better than the same author's text-only posts, and 120 to 250 words worked best. Jonathan's voice: first person, plain, British spelling. No hashtags or emoji bullets.

Founder moments: drop a photo and two or three lines on the board, we write the post, Jonathan approves it.

Review rhythm To confirm

  • About ten drafts every two weeks, on the board a week before the first one goes out.
  • Three business days to review. Changes back within one.
  • A post that isn't approved by its date doesn't go out. We move it.
  • One named approver, plus a backup while Jonathan travels.
07

What good looks like

First 30 days

  • Two batches approved and posted on their dates
  • Free report live
  • More client stories cleared

By 60 days

  • One or two client stories a week
  • More ecommerce founders among the people engaging
  • Report requests from brands that fit

By 90 days

  • Calls booked by people who read the posts or asked for a report
  • The rhythm holding with little of Jonathan's time

What we won't do

  • Publish anything without approval on the board
  • Name a client, or use their numbers without approval
  • Make up a number, a client, a quote or a story
  • Promise rankings or AI recommendations
  • Criticise a brand we're talking to
08

To decide on the call

  1. Client stories: which of the six above we can use, and which numbers.
  2. Rhythm: five posts a week, a batch every two weeks, three days to review. Who approves, and who covers during Shenzhen.
  3. Free report launch: who on your team reviews each report, the weekly cap (we suggest 15), and the date: Wednesday 21 October, or Tuesday 3 November.
  4. Teardowns: whether posts can show real AI answers about brands that aren't clients.
  5. Founder posts: travel dates, and which topics from Jonathan's life are on or off.
  6. Inputs: 10 to 20 photos by an agreed date, and emails for Lyndsay, Leah and Zeke.
